# 3B AI Business Automation — Client Flow

## Product position

3B AI Business Automation is a standalone 3B service branch and source of income. Established businesses may enter directly without using business formation or the broader 3B Business Development workflow. Clients who need business structure, licensing, or readiness support can be referred into that separate branch with appropriate consent.

## Identity and business record rule

- Every person signs in with their verified **3B ID**.
- Every client workspace is attached to a **3B Business ID**, including a sole proprietorship. A sole proprietor creates or claims the Business ID during intake so business audits, recommendations, proposals, billing, implementation records, documents, and results remain associated with the business.
- The 3B ID identifies the person; the 3B Business ID identifies the business. Do not use a personal ID as the business record or create a duplicate business for convenience.
- A person may be a member of multiple businesses. Each business record has an explicit membership and role (for example owner, administrator, staff, or 3B service team).
- The server verifies identity, business membership, and permission on every business-scoped action. Never trust a browser-supplied user or business identifier by itself.
- If a business already has a 3B Business ID, verify and link it; do not create another. If the user cannot prove authority to claim it, pause access and route to a review process.
- Use the existing 3B identity and business registry after auditing its actual contracts. Names here are product requirements, not permission to create duplicate identity tables or unsafe auth.

## Client journey

### 1. Choose an entry path

Offer two clear paths:
- **Established business:** Start an AI Automation Audit directly.
- **Building or restructuring a business:** Continue through 3B Business Development; optionally add the automation service when useful.

Both paths arrive in the same AI Business Automation service branch. Record the referral/source so branch revenue and conversions can be measured separately.

### 2. Sign in and establish the business workspace

1. Sign in or create a verified 3B ID.
2. Choose an existing 3B Business ID or create/claim one.
3. For a sole proprietor, collect the operating/business name (allowing a clear “individual/sole proprietor” designation), business type, jurisdiction, and contact details needed for the service. Explain that a Business ID is the 3B business record; it is not itself an LLC, license, tax registration, or legal determination.
4. Confirm the user’s role and authority for the business.
5. Show the selected person and business context before any business data is saved.

Do not make a public EIN or formal entity filing a prerequisite for a sole proprietor to create a Business ID. Collect sensitive identifiers only when a specific approved service truly requires them.

### 2A. Confirm the business name, location, and contact number before filing

This step is required before any formation or tax-registration paperwork is prepared or submitted. Existing businesses can use it to verify their current records and identify mismatches.

1. Ask whether the business already has a legal name, operating/DBA name, and registered entity. If the name is undecided, generate name candidates based on the services, audience, industry, geography, and brand preferences. If a name exists, review clarity and fit and flag mismatches across records.
2. Show a **recommended name** and alternatives with reasons. For preliminary screening, offer Dynadot's free USPTO trademark search as one way to look for matching federal records, before registering a domain or launching the brand. Explain its limits: it is an informational exact-record search, does not identify sound-alike/logo similarities or all state/common-law rights, and is not legal clearance. Mark state name availability, full trademark clearance, domain availability, and filing eligibility as **unverified** until checked through the relevant official sources or a qualified trademark professional. Never imply that an AI suggestion is legally available.
3. Determine where the business will actually operate and where paperwork is intended to be filed. Capture physical operating location, mailing address, registered-agent address, and principal business address as separate address types. Ask which jurisdiction the owner intends to use and why; flag inconsistencies before paperwork proceeds.
4. **Select and confirm a registered agent before preparing LLC formation paperwork.** Capture the agent's legal name, Nevada street address, consent/acceptance, availability for service of process during required business hours, and contact details. Distinguish the registered-agent appointment from the business's mailing or virtual mailbox address. For Nevada, check the Secretary of State's current requirements and official commercial-agent listing. An eligible individual or entity with a Nevada physical address may serve as a noncommercial registered agent under Nevada's rules; commercial-agent registration requirements apply at the statutory threshold. Never list a provider until that provider has agreed to serve the specific business and the current state criteria have been checked.
5. Present address options, including a physical operating address, registered-agent service, and iPostal virtual mailbox where appropriate. Explain that a virtual mailing address may not satisfy every state filing, licensing, tax, banking, lender, or funding requirement. Verify intended use against the relevant official rules and the provider's terms; do not promise acceptance. An iPostal mailbox is not automatically the registered agent; its separate registered-agent service must be selected, available for the state, and confirmed for the client.
6. Offer the user's iPostal affiliate link as a clearly disclosed optional resource when a virtual mailbox fits the client's needs. Retrieve the exact approved affiliate URL from 3B's affiliate/vendor catalog at implementation time. The existing iPostal affiliate program advertises commission on mailbox rental fees; do not represent registered-agent add-ons as commissionable unless the current terms confirm that. Track clicks/conversions only under the applicable privacy and affiliate-disclosure rules.
7. Help the client establish a dedicated business phone number using an approved provider/catalog option. Verify availability, pricing, portability, SMS/voice capabilities, emergency-calling limitations, and whether the number is suitable for the client's business, licensing, banking, and funding use. Do not claim that a VoIP number will be accepted everywhere. Record business phone separately from the owner's personal number.
8. Generate a **pre-filing consistency review** showing the proposed legal/business name, entity type, jurisdiction, business location, mailing address, registered-agent name and street address, agent acceptance, phone, industry/NAICS recommendation, tax-classification information, and any unresolved mismatch. Require client confirmation and the required human review before preparing filing paperwork. No filing or third-party purchase happens automatically.

Business-name and jurisdiction help is informational. It does not guarantee legal name availability, registration approval, address acceptance, or funding approval. Escalate ambiguous entity, tax, licensing, or address questions to the appropriate qualified professional or official agency.

### 3. Set goals and complete the audit

Capture business profile, industry, size, locations, goals, software, lead sources, process volumes, repetitive work, current pain points, and constraints. Allow “Known,” “Customer estimate,” and “Unknown” for every metric. Save progress and show why requested details matter.

### 4. Review findings and automation opportunities

Generate candidate opportunities from the shared automation catalog and industry data. For every estimate, show its source, confidence, assumptions, and ranges where appropriate. The client can correct facts, mark items unknown, and choose which goals matter most. AI drafts; a qualified 3B reviewer approves customer-facing recommendations.

### 5. Select scope and receive a proposal

Present suitable options with included automations, exclusions, implementation milestones, one-time and recurring charges, third-party software costs, assumptions, expected outcomes, and support terms. Preserve proposal versions. Authorized 3B staff review pricing and scope before sending. The client can accept, decline, request changes, or ask a question.

### 6. Approve and pay

Record approval against the proposal version. Route payment through the existing 3B Payments service after its capabilities and entitlement rules are audited. Support approved deposits, payment schedules, setup fees, and recurring service where configured. Do not collect payment details in this product or represent an estimate as guaranteed savings.

### 7. Complete implementation onboarding

Create an implementation workspace for the selected 3B Business ID. Collect only information required by the approved scope: contacts, hours, services, lead rules, brand voice, connected tools, escalation preferences, and compliance constraints. Secrets must use an approved credential flow; never request API keys in ordinary notes or chat.

### 8. Configure, test, and launch

Track requirements, configuration, development, internal testing, customer testing, approval, production launch, and monitoring. No customer-system change or production deployment occurs without the required authorization. Record test evidence, owner, environment, release version, rollback plan where applicable, and customer approval.

### 9. Report outcomes and support

Show automation health, activity, time saved, recovered leads/revenue, costs, and ROI. Label observed metrics separately from estimates and projections. Provide support and incident status, and let the client pause or request changes to a service under the agreed terms.

### 10. Recommend the next improvement

Use measured results and newly discovered needs to suggest additional automations or a separate Business Development service. Explain value, cost, dependencies, confidence, and timing. Track acceptance or dismissal; do not repeatedly surface dismissed recommendations.

### 11. Offer 3B Media Group services after business setup

When the client's business formation is filed or the existing business setup is marked complete, offer an optional **3B Media Group Small Business Online Launch Toolkit**. 3B Media Group is the provider and owns delivery of the client-facing services:

- Custom logo and brand identity design
- Domain name search, registration, and setup coordination. **Dynadot is a candidate provider** for availability search, registration, DNS management, and domain-event webhooks. Use its current REST API path; Dynadot marks the legacy command API for retirement and the REST API documentation as beta. Validate supported endpoints in Dynadot's sandbox before production use, and keep the adapter replaceable if the API changes.
- Business email setup using the client's domain, including mailbox/provider selection and DNS configuration where in scope.
- A professionally built website, with a defined starter scope such as a responsive one-page launch site, plus separately priced upgrades for additional pages, ecommerce, booking, analytics, or ongoing care.
- Basic on-page SEO setup and mobile optimization as stated deliverables in the selected package.
- Optional logo-builder or free domain-provider tools as supplemental DIY resources only; they do not replace 3B Media's custom logo, email setup, or website build.
- Optional ongoing website care or updates, if offered

Dynadot may provide a domain and eligible included starter tools. Its current site describes a free one-page builder tier with 500 MB storage and a Pro plan shown at $30/year with additional features. Treat provider inclusions, eligibility, prices, and limits as live-vendor details to recheck at purchase; the 3B Media Toolkit remains the 3B service package and clearly identifies third-party tools versus work delivered by 3B Media.

For an established business that already completed setup, show the offer when the client is ready; do not make formation a prerequisite for media services. Present the Toolkit, optional upgrades, third-party charges, 3B scope, price, timeline, and approval clearly. The client may choose the full package, selected services, or none. Domain search results are time-sensitive and are not a reservation. Before registration, show the exact domain, term, price, renewal price where available, privacy options, registrant contact, included-tool limits, and account ownership; require explicit client authorization and payment. Register domains in the client's authorized account where supported, never silently in a 3B-owned account. Store Dynadot API credentials only in approved server-side secret storage; do not expose them in the browser, logs, or customer-visible records.

With the client's consent, pass only the needed business profile, confirmed name, brand preferences, contact details, and service selections to 3B Media Group, all attached to the same 3B Business ID. Keep Media's work order, revenue, delivery status, and support ownership in the Media branch. Do not automatically register a domain, publish a website, or purchase a logo/design service; require client approval and payment through the approved shared billing flow. Confirm domain availability and ownership before purchase, and place the domain in the client's authorized account wherever supported.

## Branch ownership and reporting

3B AI Business Automation owns its automation audit, catalog, offer, proposal, onboarding, implementation, support, outcomes, and branch revenue attribution. 3B Business Development owns business foundation and formation workflows. **3B Media Group owns logo/brand design, domain services, website builds, and website care.** Shared identity, business registry, payments, API, and approved document services remain shared 3B capabilities. Referrals between branches require a recorded customer choice/consent where data is shared.

Report independently: direct automation leads, Business Development referrals, audits completed, proposals accepted, setup revenue, recurring revenue, churn, implementation delivery, support load, and measured customer outcomes.

## Core customer-facing statuses

**Workspace:** Business setup needed → Ready  
**Audit:** Not started → In progress → Ready for review → Reviewed  
**Plan:** Opportunities ready → Proposal in review → Sent → Accepted / Changes requested / Declined  
**Implementation:** Onboarding → Configuration → Testing → Customer approval → Live → Monitoring → Complete  
**Automation health:** Setup required / Healthy / Warning / Error / Paused / Disconnected

Statuses must describe actual recorded state; do not imply that a recommendation is verified, paid, deployed, or producing results before evidence exists.
